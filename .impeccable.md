## Design Context

### Users
Visa applicants (often first-time, often applying from India to Schengen/UK/US/Canada) who print or attach the itinerary to a visa application. The document's real reader is a consular officer skimming dozens of files: they need to find dates, route, and where the applicant stays in seconds.

### Brand Personality
Orderly, legible, official. It should feel like a well-made airline or travel-agency itinerary, calm and precise, never salesy or decorative.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Airline-document crisp: dense but orderly; journeys (Departure / Return) as strong headers; times and airport codes dominate; thin rules instead of boxes; one accent colour.
- Accent: navy ink. Neutrals tinted slightly toward navy. Must print well in black and white.
- Type: condensed grotesk for times, codes and headers (airport-signage feel), a sober public-sector sans for body text.
- Anti-references: boxed "card" UIs, badges everywhere, gradients, anything that looks like a marketing page. Also never imitate a booking confirmation (no booking refs, ticket numbers, prices, seats, "CONFIRMED" stamps).

### Design Principles
1. The officer's 5-second scan wins: dates, route, stays first; everything else secondary.
2. Rules, not boxes: hierarchy through type size/weight and hairlines.
3. One accent, used sparingly (journey headers, airport codes).
4. Print-first: A4, black-and-white safe, no element split across pages.
5. Honest: every flight and stay shows its real status.
