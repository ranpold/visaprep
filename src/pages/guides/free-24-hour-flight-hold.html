How to hold a flight reservation for free

If your consulate wants a reservation it can verify, you usually don't need to pay a third party. Here are the legitimate ways to get one.

1. The US 24-hour rule

US Department of Transportation rules cover flights to, from, or within the United States that are booked directly with the airline at least 7 days before departure. The airline must do one of two things:

  • hold the reservation at the quoted fare for 24 hours without payment, or
  • let you cancel within 24 hours for a full refund.

Most airlines choose free cancellation, so you pay and then get your money back if you cancel in time. A few offer a true unpaid hold. The rule applies only to bookings made directly with the airline, and only on US-touching routes.

2. Airline hold options

Some airlines outside the US let you "hold" or "lock" a fare for a day or more, either free or for a small fee. Look for "hold booking", "fare lock", or "pay later" when you book on the airline's own website.

3. Refundable or flexible fares

A fully refundable ticket is a real booking you can cancel if the visa is refused. It costs more up front, so read the refund conditions, deadlines, and any fees first.

Timing tips

  • Hold close to your appointment. A 24-hour hold is gone before most visas are processed. Many consulates accept an itinerary plan, so use holds only when a verifiable reservation is explicitly required.
  • Keep your airline's own confirmation email. That's the real proof. A reformatted "ticket" isn't.
  • Be upfront. In your cover letter, say that flights will be purchased once the visa is granted.
Always check the current rules. Airline policies and regulations change. Confirm the hold or cancellation terms on the airline's website before booking.
