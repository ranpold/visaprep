Do you need a flight ticket to apply for a visa?

Short answer: usually not. Most visa authorities ask for your travel plans. They don't ask for a paid ticket, and several say outright that you shouldn't buy one until your visa is approved.

What consulates actually ask for

  • Schengen: Most consulates list a "flight reservation" or "itinerary". Many specifically warn applicants not to buy tickets before the decision.
  • United Kingdom: GOV.UK says you don't need to book or pay for travel before you get a visa.
  • United States: The State Department advises against making final travel arrangements until you have your visa.
  • Japan: Many embassies want a written schedule of your stay, which is a day-by-day plan.

Requirements differ by consulate and nationality. Always check the official page for the office you're applying to.

The problem with "dummy tickets"

Many sites sell PDFs designed to look like airline e-tickets, sometimes with a short-lived reservation code. Here's why that's a poor deal:

  • It's a presentation risk. If a document looks like a ticket but isn't one, it can be treated as misrepresentation. Consequences range from refusal to a multi-year ban, depending on the country.
  • Officers can check. Consulates can look up reservations, and codes that expired or never existed stand out.
  • You're paying for something you can get free. A plan costs nothing, and a real hold is often free too.

The honest options

  1. A clear itinerary plan. List your intended flights and stays, and state plainly that they're planned. Our free itinerary builder does exactly this.
  2. A real reservation you hold for free. Many airlines let you hold or freely cancel within 24 hours. See our free flight hold guide.
  3. A refundable fare. These cost more up front, but you get the money back if the visa is refused. Read the fare rules carefully.

What a good itinerary includes

  • Your name exactly as it appears in your passport
  • Entry and exit dates that match your application form
  • Route, airline, and approximate times for each flight
  • Where you'll stay each night, with dates that line up with your flights
  • The status of each item (planned, held, booked)

Consistency matters more than looks. Your dates on the form, itinerary, hotel bookings, insurance, and leave letter should all match.
